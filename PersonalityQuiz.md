3\. Soud Alignment(find a better name)

The Concept: Uses Dungeons \& Dragons style moral alignment (Lawful/Chaotic, Good/Evil) to rate how safe or risky you are when handed the car AUX cord or party speaker.



Question Style: Behavioral, scenario-based questions about real-life social situations and group music settings.



How Questions Work: Questions place the user at a party, in a car, or hosting an event and present scenarios where their taste collides with group expectations. Options force a choice between playing it safe for the crowd (Lawful/Good), maintaining personal taste at all costs (Evil/Individualistic), or being completely unpredictable with queue choices (Chaotic).

Result Archetypes:



Lawful Good: "The Crowd Pleaser" (Safe hit after safe hit, zero skipped tracks)



Chaotic Neutral: "The Genre Blender" (Jumps from death metal to bossa nova without warning)



Chaotic Evil: "The Aux Saboteur" (10-minute experimental noise tracks at a chill barbecue)



4.Music Love Languages(find a better name)

The Concept: (Base: The 5 Love Languages) Identifies how you express affection and form deep personal connections through music—whether that's through detailed lyrics (Words of Acoustic Affirmation), tailored playlists (Acts of Curation), or shared concert experiences (Quality Listening Time).



Question Style: Emotional and relational questions focused on affection, sharing habits, and personal connection.



How Questions Work: Questions ask how you react when someone sends you a song, what music-related gesture makes you feel most cared for, and how you share music with others. Options map directly to the five behaviors: crafting playlists (Acts of Curation), sending specific lyric timestamps (Words of Affirmation), going to live shows together (Quality Time), buying vinyl/merch (Gift Giving), or sharing an earbud (Physical/Shared Presence).



5\. Walk in the woods (find a better name)

Concept: Adapts the classic psychological projection test (A Walk in the Woods) into an imaginary sensory journey through a sound-filled realm. Instead of choosing physical objects, users make narrative choices about sounds, volumes, and musical environments.

How It Works: Translates atmospheric narrative choices into Spotify audio metadata ($valence$, $energy$, $acousticness$, $instrumentalness$) to reveal deep emotional habits, stress responses, and intimacy styles.

Question Style: Uses vivid narrative metaphors (e.g., entering a silent forest, weathering a sudden storm, finding an unreleased track) to test baseline mood, coping mechanisms, and music-sharing instincts without asking direct questions about taste.



6.he Musical Hierarchy of Needs

Base Quiz: Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs



Concept: Maps where music sits in your daily life—from basic survival utility to spiritual self-actualization.



How It Works: Analyzes playlist types and context tags—distinguishing functional listening (workout, study beats, sleep noise) from active, deep album listening and emotional processing.



Question Style: Functional priority questions. Asks what would happen if you lost music for a week: Would your productivity crash (Physiological/Utility), would you lose your social connection (Belonging), or would you lose your sense of self (Self-Actualization)?
